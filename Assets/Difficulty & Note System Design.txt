# Reelin' In Rhythm — Difficulty & Note System Design

*Design discussion summary — living document, revisit and update as implementation evolves.*

## Core Inspiration: WebFishing

WebFishing's spam-click reeling mechanic scales required clicks with fish rarity, and if the
player's gear isn't leveled up, some fish are effectively **impossible** to catch. This makes
rare fish visible as future goals rather than hidden content, and gives the player a concrete
reason to upgrade gear.

The key insight translated for our rhythm-based system: it's not about *more notes*, it's about
**how much a correct hit is worth** relative to the fish's difficulty. This preserves felt
difficulty without requiring longer songs or infinite content.

---

## Two Independent Difficulty Axes

Rather than one blended difficulty score, the system splits into two orthogonal axes — mirroring
WebFishing's skill gate + gear gate split:

1. **Song/note-pattern difficulty (Tier)** — is this pattern hard to *play well*? (note density,
   note type variety, timing tightness)
2. **Power/progress difficulty** — even playing perfectly, can the player's gear make enough
   progress per hit to actually land the fish?

Keeping these separate means a strong player with weak gear experiences the fish differently than
a weak player with strong gear — both axes matter independently.

---

## Axis 1: Progress-Per-Hit (Power Gate)

Instead of scaling hit *count*, scale how much **progress** each successful hit contributes to
the fish meter:

```
difficultyScore = rarityBaseDifficulty × fishDifficultyMultiplier
progressPerHit  = playerPower / difficultyScore
```

- `rarityBaseDifficulty` — derived from the existing `RarityConfig` curve (Common = low,
  Legendary = high).
- `fishDifficultyMultiplier` — a per-fish override field, letting a specific fish be harder than
  its rarity would suggest (e.g. a stubborn Common fish).
- `playerPower` — a new derived stat (possibly a dedicated `ReelPower` stat on `PlayerStats`,
  combined with equipped gear bonuses).

**Why not just add more required hits per fish:** a song has a fixed note count; scaling hit
count either requires ever-longer songs (limited content) or repeating the same short loop
multiple times (repetitive, breaks pacing). Scaling *progress per hit* achieves the same felt
difficulty without touching song/pattern structure.

**Other levers that can scale with `difficultyScore`** (not just progress-per-hit):
- **Miss penalty** — `FishMeter.Decay()` amount could scale up with difficulty, rewarding
  consistency, not just raw power.
- **Hit window tightness** — the flat 15° hit window could shrink for harder fish, adding a
  precision gate alongside the power gate.

**Open design question (unresolved):** should low power ever make a fish *literally impossible*
(0% chance, like WebFishing), or always *technically possible but brutally unforgiving* (near-zero
progress per hit, demanding a flawless run)? Bold vs. polarizing tradeoff — revisit before
finalizing.

---

## Axis 2: Difficulty Tier (Skill Gate)

**Final structure (as decided):** Keep the existing 3 lists per song (`Easy` / `Medium` / `Hard`
inside `NoteHitTimings`), but each tier is now **self-contained and independently clearable** —
no more layering tiers on top of each other. A fish encounter picks **one** tier to play, not a
stacked combination.

- No tier should ever be unwinnable. The difference between tiers is **more simultaneous
  responsibilities** — higher note density, more/varied note types (Tap, Hold, future types),
  tighter execution demands — not impossible content.
- This also resolves an existing bug: `NoteHitManager.SpawnNotesOnTime()` currently only ever
  reads `spawnTimes[0]` (Easy), even though `GetSpawnTimes()` loads all three lists — the
  "harder fish layer in more lists" idea was never actually implemented. The new model replaces
  this mechanism entirely rather than patching the bug.

**Tier resolution formula** (uses the *same* underlying numbers as progress-per-hit, just
consumed differently):

```
relativeDifficulty = fishDifficultyScore / playerPower
tier = threshold mapping of relativeDifficulty → Easy / Medium / Hard
```

Using the same `fishDifficultyScore` / `playerPower` relationship for both axes means there's
only one core relationship to balance-tune, not two disconnected curves.

**Structural simplification this enables:** `NoteHitManager.spawnTimes` no longer needs to hold
all three lists (`List<List<NoteTiming>>`) — since only one tier's notes ever spawn per encounter,
it can become a single resolved `List<NoteTiming>`, chosen once in `GetSpawnTimes()` based on the
resolved tier.

**Open question:** should specific fish be able to override the tier resolution (e.g. "this fish
always plays at least Medium," similar to the per-fish difficulty multiplier)? Not yet decided.

---

## Song Selection (Decoupled from Difficulty)

Original idea (superseded): give each song a `difficultyRating` and pick a song near the fish's
difficulty score.

**Current direction:** since difficulty now lives entirely in the *tier* (Axis 2), song selection
no longer needs to encode difficulty at all. Song choice becomes a pure **variety/flavor**
decision — random from the library, or later tied to location/fish-type — fully decoupled from
challenge level. This is simpler than the difficulty-rating-per-song approach and means the same
song can be reused across many fish encounters at different difficulties, without needing 3x the
total songs.

---

## Net Encounter Flow (resulting model)

A fish encounter resolves in two independent steps:

1. **Pick a song** — flavor/variety only, no difficulty implication.
2. **Pick a tier from that song** (Easy/Medium/Hard) — driven by `fishDifficultyScore` vs.
   `playerPower`, determines which pre-authored, guaranteed-clearable note list plays.

Progress-per-hit (Axis 1) then determines how much each successful hit within that tier actually
advances the fish meter, independent of which tier or song was chosen.

---

## Not Yet Decided / Follow-Up Topics

- Impossible vs. always-technically-possible fish at low power (Axis 1 open question above).
- Per-fish tier override / minimum tier floor (Axis 2 open question above).
- Exact `playerPower` stat design — new dedicated stat vs. derived from existing `PlayerStats`.
- Threshold values for `relativeDifficulty → tier` mapping.
- Whether miss-penalty scaling and hit-window tightening (secondary levers) get implemented
  alongside progress-per-hit, or added later as extra tuning knobs.
